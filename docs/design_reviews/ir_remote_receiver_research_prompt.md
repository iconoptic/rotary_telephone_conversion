# Deep research request: recommend IR remote-receiver components for a >30cm remote trigger

## Your role

Act as two specialists working together:

1. An **optoelectronics/component-selection engineer** who knows demodulating IR remote-control receiver ICs (the "TSOP-class" 3-lead parts), their carrier-frequency/AGC behavior, and how to read/verify their datasheets.
2. An **embedded hobbyist-electronics reviewer** who can weigh hobbyist buildability (THT vs SMD, breadboard-friendliness, hobby-distributor stock) alongside the raw electrical spec, for someone building this on a Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040/MicroPython) with basic tools and no reflow equipment.

Cite the manufacturer's own datasheet for every part you recommend. Do not guess distributor product-page URLs or part numbers — if you cannot verify a specific distributor listing, say so plainly rather than presenting a guess as fact (this project has been burned by exactly that failure mode before, on both bell-transformer and MOSFET part research).

## What just happened (the trigger for this request)

This project's rotary phone has an existing bare 2-lead IR emitter/receiver pair (see wiring below) built as a **reflective proximity sensor**: it flashes its own emitter and looks for the reflection off a nearby hand to trigger the bell. A recent audit (prompted by the user attaching the actual component's datasheet/wiring sheet) confirmed that this circuit is architecturally incapable of a *different*, newly-clarified requirement: detecting a **separate, independent IR remote transmitter from more than 30cm away**, indoors, under fluorescent/LED lighting — not a hand, not a reflection.

The reason is fundamental, not a tuning problem: the firmware samples the receiver with its own emitter forced off/on/off and subtracts, specifically to cancel out everything that ISN'T its own emitter's reflection. A steady remote light source cancels out exactly; an unsynchronized modulated one just looks like noise. No amount of current, resistor, or threshold tuning fixes this — the existing bare receiver needs to be supplemented (not necessarily replaced) with a proper demodulating IR receiver IC for the remote-detection job specifically.

## What we already know / already decided (do not re-litigate these)

- **Platform:** Raspberry Pi Pico, RP2040, MicroPython, 3.3V logic throughout. GP26/ADC0 is NOT 5V-tolerant (abs max = 3.3V). Any new receiver's output must be safe to feed directly into a 3.3V GPIO input (open-collector active-low into an internal pull-up is fine; push-pull 0–3.3V/5V logic needs checking against the RP2040's input abs-max).
- **Pins already committed** (do not conflict with these): GP2 (dial shunt), GP3 (dial pulse), GP4 (switchhook), GP14/15/16 (status LEDs), GP17/18 (bell FET gate drive), GP19 (existing IR emitter, GPIO output), GP26/ADC0 (existing bare IR receiver, analog input). Any new part should assume it gets its own fresh, currently-unused GPIO as a **digital** input.
- **The existing reflective sensor (GP19 emitter + bare receiver on GP26) stays as-is** for its original job (hand-wave proximity trigger for the bell). This request is for an ADDITIONAL receiver, not a replacement, unless you have a strong reason to argue otherwise (say so explicitly if you do).
- **A known trap already identified, do not repeat it as a "discovery":** many common 38kHz demodulating receiver ICs (this project's docs have repeatedly named the TSOP38238 as a hypothetical upgrade path) have automatic gain control (AGC) that actively **suppresses a continuous, un-bursted carrier** — a naive remote built from a single free-running 555 timer blasting steady 38kHz will often be ignored or only fire once. Any recommendation must explicitly address this: either (a) recommend parts specifically rated/marketed for continuous-carrier ("light barrier") use, or (b) confirm the remote-transmitter side must burst the carrier (data pulses, not a steady tone) and explain the minimum burst/gap timing that keeps a normal AGC-equipped receiver happy.
- **Range/environment target:** reliable detection at **>30cm**, indoors, under fluorescent or LED lighting (not direct sunlight, not outdoors). Favor parts/designs with good ambient-light immunity in that environment over ones optimized for direct sunlight rejection specifically.
- **Existing emitter is 940nm** (Gikfun EK8443, bare 2-lead, Vf 1.2–1.5V) — note whether your recommended receiver's spectral response is a good match for 940nm specifically, since many demodulating IR receivers are centered closer to 950nm.

## Specific questions to answer

1. **Recommend a variety of suitable demodulating IR receiver ICs** (not just one part) for this job, spanning at least these categories, each with 2–3 concrete candidate part numbers:
   - Conventional bursted-protocol remote-control receivers (e.g., the TSOP48xx/TSOP38xxx/TSOP4838-style family, or equivalents from Vishay, Everlight, Osram, Liteon) — note which specific carrier frequency each is tuned for (36kHz/38kHz/40kHz/56kHz etc.) and whether datasheet AGC behavior is documented.
   - Continuous-carrier-tolerant "light barrier" / industrial-style parts explicitly rated to NOT suppress a steady carrier, if such parts exist in an easy-to-source, hobbyist-friendly package.
   - Any notably different approach worth considering (e.g., a bare photodiode + dedicated demodulator/decoder chip, or a "smart" IR receiver module breakout board with onboard decoding) — include it only if it's a genuinely reasonable alternative for a hobbyist, not just for completeness.
2. **For every recommended part**, extract and tabulate from the manufacturer's own datasheet (cite it): package type (THT 3-lead vs SMD), supply voltage range and whether 3.3V is inside spec, typical/max supply current, output logic type (open-collector active-low vs push-pull, idle-high or idle-low), carrier frequency and its tolerance/bandwidth, demodulated output pulse-width behavior, recommended decoupling (many of these parts explicitly require an RC filter or ferrite bead on Vcc to avoid false triggering — call this out if the datasheet mentions it), viewing angle, and any stated continuous-carrier / AGC caveat.
3. **Range/sensitivity check**: for each candidate, does the datasheet give (or allow you to reasonably estimate from) a typical detection distance for a matched emitter at a stated drive current? Flag if a part's typical rated range is well under 30cm and would need an unusually strong emitter to compensate, versus one that comfortably exceeds 30cm at modest emitter drive.
4. **Remote-transmitter side**: for your top 1–2 receiver recommendations, describe the minimum viable transmitter design that would reliably trigger them — realistic options to compare:
   - A minimal from-scratch circuit (e.g., two 555 timers, or a single small MCU) bursting the emitter at the receiver's carrier frequency in the on/off pattern its AGC expects.
   - Whether an existing, off-the-shelf universal remote or a spare TV/appliance remote (already emitting a standard bursted 36–40kHz protocol like NEC, RC5, or SIRC) could be repurposed as the trigger source instead of building a custom transmitter, and if so which protocol families are most likely to "just work" against your recommended receiver without protocol decoding (i.e., presence/any-signal detection only, not decoding a specific command).
5. **Final recommendation**: pick one receiver part as the primary recommendation for this project specifically (hobbyist build, RP2040/MicroPython, >30cm indoor, wants to reuse a simple/cheap remote if possible), explain why, and give one clearly-labeled fallback part in case the primary is out of stock or hard to source. Include package/pin-out basics (which pin is Vcc/GND/OUT — these 3-lead parts are notorious for non-standard pinouts between manufacturers, so do not assume a "standard" TSOP pinout applies to a different brand without checking that exact datasheet).

## Ground rules

- This is a component-recommendation task, not a request to finalize a full remote-control schematic — firmware/protocol decoding and exact resistor/capacitor values for the transmitter are out of scope for this pass; focus on picking the right receiver IC(s) and giving enough transmitter-side context to know whether an existing remote could work.
